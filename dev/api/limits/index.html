Limits and colimits

Limits and colimits of finite sets.

JuliaTopology.CoproductSet — Type
CoproductSet(D)

The disjoint union of the sets of a diagram D of finite sets, as a lazy finite set: its elements are (vertex = j, element = x) for each vertex j and element x of D(j). Built by colimit.

source
JuliaTopology.LimitSet — Type
LimitSet(D)

The limit of a diagram D of finite sets, as a lazy finite set: its elements are the NamedTuples (A = a, B = b, …) with an element of D(j) for each vertex j, such that D(f)(x_j) == x_k for each arrow f: j → k. Its elements are computed when it is iterated, not stored. Built by limit.

source
JuliaTopology.QuotientSet — Type
QuotientSet(representatives, D)

The colimit of a diagram D of finite sets, as the set of one representative (vertex = j, element = x) of each class of the disjoint union of its sets. The classes themselves are given by the surjection built alongside it in colimit.

source
JuliaTopology.coequalizer — Method
coequalizer(f, g)

The coequalizer of maps f, g: X → Y of finite sets, the colimit of parallelPair(f, g): a cocone whose apex is Y divided by the smallest equivalence relating f(x) and g(x) for each x.

source
JuliaTopology.colimit — Method
colimit(D)

The colimit of a diagram D of finite sets, an initial object of Cocone(D). Its apex is the QuotientSet of the disjoint union of the sets of D by the smallest equivalence relating (j, x) and (k, D(f)(x)) for each arrow f: j → k, and its leg at j sends x to the class of (j, x). For any cocone c under D, canonicalHom(colimit(D), c) is the unique morphism of cocones out of it. The classes are computed from the sets as they are when colimit is called, and each call builds a new cocone.

source
JuliaTopology.coproduct — Method
coproduct(X₁, X₂, …)
coproduct(; A = X₁, B = X₂, …)

The coproduct of finite sets, the colimit of discrete(…): a cocone whose apex is their disjoint union and whose legs are the inclusions.

source
JuliaTopology.equalizer — Method
equalizer(f, g)

The equalizer of maps f, g: X → Y of finite sets, the limit of parallelPair(f, g): a cone whose apex has an element (X = x, Y = f(x)) for each x with f(x) == g(x).

source
JuliaTopology.initial — Method
initial(FinSet)

The initial finite set, the apex of the colimit of the empty diagram, which is empty. canonicalHom(initial(FinSet), X) is the unique map out of it.

source
JuliaTopology.limit — Method
limit(D)

The limit of a diagram D of finite sets, a terminal object of Cone(D): the cone whose apex is the LimitSet of D and whose legs are its projections, p ↦ object(p)[j]. For any cone c over D, canonicalHom(c, limit(D)) is the unique morphism of cones into it.

source
JuliaTopology.product — Method
product(X₁, X₂, …)
product(; A = X₁, B = X₂, …)

The product of finite sets, the limit of discrete(…): a cone whose apex has the elements (X1 = x₁, X2 = x₂, …), or (A = x₁, B = x₂, …), and whose legs are the projections.

source
JuliaTopology.pullback — Method
pullback(f, g)

The pullback of maps f: A → C and g: B → C of finite sets, the limit of cospan(f, g): a cone whose apex has an element (A = a, B = b, C = f(a)) for each a, b with f(a) == g(b).

source
JuliaTopology.pushout — Method
pushout(f, g)

The pushout of maps f: C → A and g: C → B of finite sets, the colimit of span(f, g): a cocone whose apex is the disjoint union of A and B, with f(c) and g(c) identified for each c.

source
JuliaTopology.terminal — Method
terminal(FinSet)

The terminal finite set, the apex of the limit of the empty diagram, whose only element is (). canonicalHom(X, terminal(FinSet)) is the unique map into it.
